What does a web app cost?

Honestly, it depends on what the app does. Rather than quote a range that fits no one, this guide explains what actually shapes the cost, so you can plan, prioritise and compare quotes with confidence.

The biggest factors

1. How many types of user

An app used only by your team is simpler than one where customers, staff and admins each have their own screens, permissions and journeys. Every extra type of user adds design, development and testing.

2. The features and the rules behind them

“Bookings” can mean a simple list of slots, or availability rules, waiting lists, cancellations, refunds and reminders. Your business rules often take more work than the screens themselves.

3. Connections to other software

Linking to payment providers, accounting packages, calendars or your existing systems depends on what those systems allow. Some connections are straightforward and others need careful work and testing.

4. Your existing data

Moving records from spreadsheets or an old system means mapping, cleaning and checking the data. It’s often underestimated.

5. Offline use and device features

Making parts of an app work without signal, and syncing changes safely afterwards, is specialist work. Push notifications, camera use and location also add development and testing across devices.

6. Design detail

A clean, accessible interface is part of every project. Bespoke illustrations, complex animation or highly custom visual design add to it.

Costs after launch

Every app has some running costs. Plan for them from the start:

  • Hosting on a reliable cloud platform, scaled to how many people use the app.
  • Your domain name and any email-sending service.
  • Third-party services such as payment provider fees or software subscriptions.
  • Maintenance: security and software updates, backups and fixes.
  • Improvements as you learn what your users need.

Where a web app can help the budget

One web app can serve phones, tablets and computers from a single codebase, rather than separate native apps for each platform. Updates go live on the server without app store releases. For many projects that means less to build and less to maintain, but the real figure always depends on your scope.

Ways to keep the cost under control

  • Start with a first version. Build the features that deliver most of the value, launch, then add more based on real feedback.
  • Use existing services where they fit. Payments, email delivery and maps are better handled by established providers than built from scratch.
  • Agree a written scope. A clear scope means fewer surprises for both of us.
  • Be clear on what matters most. If something has to be ready by a date, we can plan the build around it.
